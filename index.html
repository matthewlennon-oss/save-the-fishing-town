Your mission

A fictional Australian coastal town faces falling fish populations.

Local families rely on fishing, but years of heavy catches have reduced the number of fish in the bay. As the council's sustainability adviser, you must protect marine life, keep livelihoods secure and support a healthy local economy. You get two rounds to try.

How does the classroom model work?

Each plan starts with a fictional fish population index of 42/100. The model projects five years. Protected waters reduce the area available to fishing, supporting fish recovery but reducing fishing access. Fishing intensity increases fishing activity in open waters and can support current jobs and income, but excessive fishing pressure reduces fish stocks. Community support helps families move into new jobs and diversifies the economy, but has a short-term cost.

This revision deliberately makes maximising all three levers a poor strategy: 100% protection leaves no legal fishing grounds, 100% community support is expensive and provides diminishing additional benefit, and 100% fishing intensity increases pressure only on whatever area remains open. Some intermediate strategies can reach all four targets.

The four output scores are invented educational indices, not measured fish numbers, employment rates, dollar amounts, or predictions. The model is deliberately deterministic: identical choices produce identical results. The threshold values are learning targets, not real management standards. Actual fisheries depend on species, reproduction, habitat, climate, illegal fishing, regulation, tourism, markets and much more. Marine reserves can help fisheries in some circumstances, but outcomes vary; neither fishing bans nor greater catches are guaranteed to achieve the modelled benefits.
